I did a proper production-style test across **4 companies and multiple report generations**:

* **US:** Microsoft, Coca-Cola
* **UK:** BP, Shell

I tested dedicated sustainability repositories, official corporate archives/CDNs, report-year continuity, direct-PDF behavior, report-type changes, and whether the architecture can realistically support **hundreds of documents with minimal HTTP overhead**.

## Result

For your exact requirement—**US + UK, 2017–2025, sustainability/ESG/CSR reports, maximum speed**—I would build around:

| Rank | Source                                      | Production role                                                    |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 🥇   | **SustainabilityReports.com Bulk Portal**   | Mass discovery + **bulk PDF ZIP download**                         |
| 🥈   | **Official corporate report archives/CDNs** | Authoritative direct-PDF fallback                                  |
| 🥉   | **Corporate Register CR Data Packages**     | Bulk metadata/completeness cross-check                             |
| 4    | ResponsibilityReports.com                   | Useful PDF mirror/fallback, not my primary mass-ingestion backbone |

### 1. SustainabilityReports.com Bulk Portal is the closest thing to “speed of light”

This is fundamentally different from scraping.

Their current database advertises **470,000+ reports, 62,000+ companies, 170+ countries and 160+ markets**, updated weekly. ([SustainabilityReports.com][1])

More importantly, their professional portal explicitly supports:

```text
country = United States / United Kingdom
years = 2017–2025
report type = Sustainability / ESG / CSR / Integrated
language = English
        ↓
select hundreds/thousands
        ↓
ONE BULK DOWNLOAD
        ↓
ZIP
+
metadata
```

Their portal calls this **“Instant Bulk Download”**, with advanced filtering and custom report selections. ([SustainabilityReports.com][2])

That is the only source I tested where the intended architecture itself is:

> **many PDFs → one/few bulk download objects**

rather than:

> 500 PDFs → 500 separate GET requests.

That matters enormously.

If 500 reports become one ZIP:

```text
500 reports
      ↓
1 ZIP request
      ↓
local SSD
      ↓
parallel unzip
      ↓
500 PDFs
```

Your bottleneck becomes mostly **bytes/sec**, not HTTP-request latency.

Their terms explicitly prohibit unauthorized automated bulk downloading from the normal site, so production use should go through the authorized bulk service/portal. ([SustainabilityReports.com][3])

## 🇺🇸 Microsoft

Microsoft's official Reports Hub is extremely well structured.

It currently exposes archived sustainability-related reporting including:

* Environmental Sustainability Reports for 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025 and 2026.
* CSR / Impact reporting continuously from **2017 through 2025**. ([Microsoft][4])

I also followed actual archive links.

### 2019 test

The 2019 CSR link resolves directly to:

```text
cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/
...
Microsoft-2019-CSR-Annual-Report-0.pdf
```

It is a real **40-page PDF**, not a redirect/search wrapper. ([Microsoft][5])

The report itself includes environmental sustainability as part of the CSR disclosure. ([Microsoft][5])

### 2018 test

Same pattern:

```text
cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/
...
2018-Microsoft-CSR-Report-Highlights-0.pdf
```

again a directly accessible PDF. 

### Microsoft finding

The naming/type evolves:

```text
2017 ─┐
2018  │
2019  ├─ Corporate Social Responsibility
2020 ─┘

2021+
    ↓
Impact Summary
+
Environmental Sustainability Report
```

So your crawler **cannot insist that the filename contains `sustainability-report`**.

Normalize:

```text

ESG Report

        ↓

SR_SLOT
```

depending on your project's rules.

The SustainabilityReports database also has a Microsoft company record spanning **2017–2025**, with multiple reports in many years. ([SustainabilityReports.com][6])

# 🇺🇸 Coca-Cola

This was an especially useful test because it proves report formats change.

### 2018

Coca-Cola published a proper:

**2018 Business & Sustainability Report**

and the official company CDN exposes the PDF directly. ([Coca-Cola Company][7])

### 2021

The report evolved into:

**Business and ESG Report**

The sustainability repository identifies it as an integrated report, **86 pages / ~26.13 MB**. ([SustainabilityReports.com][8])

### 2022

Coca-Cola then published a:

**2022 Business & Sustainability Report**

and the official corporate site exposes its PDF directly. ([Coca-Cola Company][9])

### 2023–2025

The company's reporting structure changes again.

Its current official sustainability page exposes:

```text
2023 Environmental Update

2024 Environmental Update
2024 Portfolio Update
2024 People & Communities Update

2025 Environmental Update
2025 Portfolio Update
2025 People & Communities Update
```

rather than necessarily one monolithic sustainability report. ([Coca-Cola Company][10])

I followed those links too.

The **2023 Environmental Update** is a direct **12-page PDF**. ([Coca-Cola Company][11])

The **2024 Environmental Update** is a direct **14-page PDF**. ([Coca-Cola Company][12])

This is exactly why production collection requires a classification engine.

For Coca-Cola:

```text
2018
Business & Sustainability

2019/2020
business/sustainability-family disclosures

2021
Business & ESG

2022
Business & Sustainability

2023+
Environmental / Portfolio / People updates
```

If your research definition says:

> one standalone sustainability report per company-year

then some newer Coca-Cola years may legitimately be:

```text
NO_SINGLE_STANDALONE_SR
```

rather than forcing one of the updates into the slot.

Interestingly, SustainabilityReports.com currently has **31 Coca-Cola reports**, but its year coverage is not identical to the company's live site—for example its company index lists 2024 and 2022 but does not show 2023 as a company-year button, whereas Coca-Cola's own site clearly has a 2023 Environmental Update. ([SustainabilityReports.com][13])

**Important production lesson:** aggregator first, official fallback second.

# What the testing tells us about raw speed

There are two completely different ingestion models.

## Model A — individual website crawling

Suppose:

```text
500 reports
```

You need approximately:

```text
500 GET requests
```

Even with 50–100 concurrent connections:

```text
DNS
TLS
HTTP latency
server throttling
redirects
file-transfer time
```

remain involved.

You can make it fast, but **“hundreds in a few seconds” cannot be guaranteed.**

---

# Model B — bulk archive delivery

This is what you want.

```text
500 reports
       ↓
bulk server
       ↓
1–few ZIPs
       ↓
SSD
       ↓
unzip locally
```

Now:

```text
HTTP request overhead ≈ almost irrelevant

speed ≈ ZIP size / bandwidth
```

This is why **SustainabilityReports.com's authorized bulk service** is the strongest match to your requirement. It explicitly offers bulk PDF delivery rather than forcing you to engineer mass scraping yourself. ([SustainabilityReports.com][26])

## For maximum speed

Do **not** do this:

```text
company
→ Google
→ open website
→ search sustainability
→ click report
→ download
→ next company
```

Do:

```text
STEP 1
Get entire metadata universe.

STEP 2
Resolve every target URL before downloading anything.

STEP 3
Manifest contains:
100,000 URLs.

STEP 4
Use bulk ZIP where available.

STEP 5
For official-CDN leftovers:
100 global async connections
but only 2–5 per host.

STEP 6
Stream directly to SSD.

STEP 7
Validate/hash in parallel.
```

# Final production verdict

For **hundreds/thousands of reports**:

**Primary:** **SustainabilityReports.com authorized Bulk Portal**

Use filters and receive **bulk ZIP + metadata**. This minimizes network transactions and is therefore the architecture with the highest theoretical throughput. ([SustainabilityReports.com][26])

**Authoritative fallback:** **official company sustainability archives/CDNs**.

The four-company test proved that Microsoft, Coca-Cola, BP and Shell all expose real report files through static corporate/CDN infrastructure, but report names and report types change over time. ([Microsoft][4])

**Completeness auditor:** **Corporate Register CR Data Package**.

It is designed for intensive professional datasets and can match an externally supplied universe using identifiers such as CIK/ISIN and return report-type/year metadata. ([Corporate Register][28])

The most important finding from the heavy test is that **speed is no longer the difficult part once URLs are resolved**. The difficult part is accurately determining whether each company-year contains a standalone SR, CSR/ESG report, integrated report, or only topic-specific disclosures. For your project, the agent should therefore build the entire **2017–2025 URL manifest first**, then the actual download stage can be extremely aggressive and simple.
